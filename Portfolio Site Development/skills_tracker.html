Causal Inference & Research Methods

The gap between descriptive analysis and causal claims is the gap between a good analyst and a credible one.
Critical
Difference-in-Differences (DiD)
Developing
The workhorse of applied policy evaluation. Every policy analyst job will expect you to recognize when DiD is appropriate, explain the parallel trends assumption, and interpret a two-way fixed effects model.
Methods Portfolio Interview
How to develop: Scott Cunningham — Causal Inference: The Mixtape (Ch. 8–9, free online). Replicate a published DiD study using R and fixest. Add one DiD analysis to your portfolio.
Critical
Regression Discontinuity (Sharp & Fuzzy)
Developing
You already have an RD project (WIC). Deepen your ability to explain the design — the running variable, the bandwidth, local average treatment effects — fluently and without jargon. This is a key interview differentiator.
Methods Interview
How to develop: Imbens & Lemieux (2008) — Regression Discontinuity Designs. rdrobust package in R. Practice explaining your WIC RD design in plain language to a non-statistician.
High
Instrumental Variables (IV)
Queued
IV is conceptually important even if you don't run it regularly — it forces clarity about endogeneity and the conditions under which you can make causal claims. Understanding its logic deepens all your other methods work.
Methods Interview
How to develop: Angrist & Pischke — Mostly Harmless Econometrics (Ch. 4). The ivreg package in R. Understand the exclusion restriction before any implementation.
High
Synthetic Control Method
Queued
Increasingly used in policy evaluation when DiD assumptions don't hold. Useful when studying state-level or city-level policy changes with a single treated unit. Growing presence in public policy literature.
Methods Portfolio
How to develop: Abadie (2021) — Using Synthetic Controls (JEL). Synth package in R. Identify a state or city policy you care about and apply it.
High
Survey Weighting & Complex Sample Analysis
Learning
Your ADA-PARC and mood disorders work both use survey data (ACS, CDC/BRFSS). Ignoring survey weights produces wrong standard errors and wrong point estimates. This is a methodological hygiene issue that reviewers catch.
Methods Portfolio
How to develop: Thomas Lumley — Complex Surveys: A Guide to Analysis Using R. The survey and srvyr packages in R. Re-run your CDC analysis with proper weights.
Medium
Fixed Effects & Panel Data Methods
Queued
Panel data (multiple units over time) is common in policy research. Fixed effects control for unobserved unit-level confounders. The fixest package in R makes this fast and elegant.
Methods Portfolio
How to develop: fixest package vignettes. Wooldridge — Introductory Econometrics (Ch. 13–14). Seek out a longitudinal policy dataset in your domain.
Medium
Power Analysis & Sample Size Planning
Queued
Expected in grant applications, IRB submissions, and any prospective research design. Being able to run and explain a power calculation demonstrates research design credibility.
Methods Interview
How to develop: pwr package in R. Gelman & Hill — Data Analysis Using Regression (Ch. 20). Practice with a hypothetical study in your domain.
Medium
Meta-Analysis & Systematic Review Literacy
Queued
Not about conducting meta-analyses yourself — about being able to read, evaluate, and cite them correctly. Systematic reviews are the gold standard of evidence synthesis in health policy. You need to know how to use them and what their limits are.
Methods Domain
How to develop: Cochrane Handbook (free online). Read 5–10 Cochrane reviews in your domain areas. Understand heterogeneity, funnel plots, and GRADE evidence ratings.

R, Quarto & Technical Tools

Tools that make your analysis faster, cleaner, and more reproducible — and your outputs more professional.
Critical
Publication-Quality Tables (gt / gtsummary)
Developing
Your ADA-PARC report demonstrates strong descriptive analysis, but your data communication can be elevated dramatically with well-designed tables. gt and gtsummary are the gold standard in R for this. A portfolio piece with a beautiful summary table reads as more professional immediately.
Tools Portfolio Communication
How to develop: gt package documentation (gt.rstudio.com). gtsummary package vignettes. Reproduce any table from your ADA-PARC report using gt as a practice exercise.
Critical
Advanced ggplot2 (publication-ready visualization)
Developing
Data visualization is a core communication skill in policy research. Your portfolio currently skews toward tables. Even one or two strong, well-designed figures dramatically expand your audience and demonstrate communication range.
Tools Portfolio Communication
How to develop: Claus Wilke — Fundamentals of Data Visualization (free online). ggplot2 extensions: ggtext, patchwork, ggthemes. Kieran Healy — Data Visualization: A Practical Introduction.
High
Quarto Publishing & GitHub Pages
Mastered
You already use this well. The value here is in continuing to refine the site and being able to speak confidently about Quarto as a reproducible publishing tool — increasingly valued in research-facing orgs.
Tools Portfolio
How to develop further: Explore Quarto features you haven't used: callouts, code folding, cross-references, freeze for heavy computations. Consider adding a blog or notes section.
High
Spatial Analysis (sf / tmap / spdep)
Queued
Your ADA-PARC work operates at national, regional, state, and city scales — it is crying out for a map. Spatial visualization instantly communicates geographic inequality in ways tables cannot. This is also a highly valued skill in urban planning and policy contexts.
Tools Portfolio Methods
How to develop: Lovelace, Nowosad & Muenchow — Geocomputation with R (free online). sf + tmap packages. Start by mapping one finding from ADA-PARC at the state level.
High
Workflow Pipelines (targets package)
Queued
Reproducibility is a core value signal in your portfolio. The targets package transforms an R project from a collection of scripts into a dependency-tracked pipeline — like Make for data analysis. Used in serious research orgs and valued by faculty evaluating doctoral applicants.
Tools Portfolio
How to develop: Will Landau — targets package manual (books.ropensci.org/targets). Convert one existing project to use targets. This signals pipeline thinking.
Medium
Tidymodels (ML Workflows in R)
Queued
Prediction-focused analysis is increasingly expected in applied policy roles — identifying at-risk populations, prioritizing program targeting, forecasting demand. tidymodels provides a coherent framework for this in R, consistent with your existing tidyverse fluency.
Tools Methods
How to develop: Kuhn & Silge — Tidy Modeling with R (free online). Focus on regularized regression (lasso, ridge) and cross-validation before more complex models.
Medium
API Data Access (Census, BLS, FRED in R)
Queued
Being able to pull data programmatically — rather than downloading CSVs — makes your analyses more reproducible and more impressive to reviewers. tidycensus, censusapi, blsR, and fredr are the key R packages for policy-relevant administrative data.
Tools Portfolio
How to develop: Kyle Walker — Analyzing US Census Data (free online, uses tidycensus). Replace any manual CSV download in your ADA-PARC workflow with a tidycensus API call.
Ongoing
tidyverse Fluency (dplyr, tidyr, purrr)
Mastered
Core competency — maintain and deepen. Focus next on functional programming patterns (purrr::map) for applying operations across multiple datasets or geographies simultaneously — directly applicable to multi-scale analysis like ADA-PARC.
Tools
How to develop further: Hadley Wickham — R for Data Science (2e, free online). Deepen purrr usage for iteration. Explore tidyr pivoting and nesting patterns.

Writing & Communication

The ability to translate rigorous analysis into clear, audience-appropriate prose is a career-defining skill in policy research.
Critical
Policy Brief Writing
Learning
A policy brief is the single most valuable missing asset in your portfolio. It demonstrates you can operate in the deliberative world — that you know what decision-makers need, how they read, and what they'll act on. Every research-adjacent role expects this capability.
Communication Portfolio Interview
How to develop: Read 10 policy briefs from RWJF, Urban Institute, Brookings, or RAND. Note their structure: problem → evidence → options → recommendation. Write your own from existing ADA-PARC or mood disorders findings. Target: 1,000–1,500 words.
Critical
Findings-First Narrative (Inverted Pyramid)
Developing
Academic writing buries the lede. Policy and research communication demands the opposite — state the finding, then explain how you know, then explain why it matters. Rewriting your project pages in this mode is the single fastest way to improve your portfolio's impact.
Communication Portfolio
How to develop: Read Urban Institute's writing style guides (free online). Practice by writing a one-paragraph project summary that begins with the finding, not the data source or research question.
High
Oral Research Narration (Non-Technical)
Developing
Every interview asks some version of "tell me about your research." The ability to explain your work — the problem, the question, the finding, the implication — in 90 seconds to a non-statistician is a skill that must be practiced. It does not come naturally from writing.
Communication Interview
How to develop: Record yourself explaining each project in 90 seconds. Listen back. Repeat until it sounds natural, not read. Practice with someone outside your field.
High
Stakeholder Presentation Design
Queued
A slide deck for a community meeting, city council briefing, or funder presentation is a completely different product from an academic report. It requires different design logic — fewer words, more visuals, one idea per slide, and a clear ask. Having one in your portfolio is a significant differentiator.
Communication Portfolio
How to develop: Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic — Storytelling with Data. Build a 6–8 slide version of ADA-PARC findings for a hypothetical city council briefing. Ask: what decision does this enable?
High
Academic Writing (Methods Sections)
Developing
If the doctoral path is in play, you need to write methods sections that are precise enough to be replicated — explaining the design, the data, the estimation strategy, and the assumptions clearly and concisely. This is a different register from policy writing.
Communication Portfolio
How to develop: Read 5–10 methods sections from leading journals in your field (JPAM, JAMA, Urban Studies). Rewrite your own methods sections in this style. Get feedback from a faculty member or advisor.
Medium
Op-Ed & Public-Facing Writing
Queued
Writing one op-ed or public commentary (even for a campus paper or local outlet) demonstrates you can translate research for a general audience and are comfortable with public intellectual engagement — valued in think tank and advocacy-adjacent roles.
Communication Portfolio
How to develop: Read The Op-Ed Project (theopedproject.org) for format guidance. Draft one 600-word piece from an existing project. Target a campus news outlet or local policy organization's blog.

Substantive Domain Knowledge

The normative and empirical depth that gives your analysis purpose — and your arguments authority.
Critical
Health Equity Literature (Core Texts & Frameworks)
Developing
Your three projects touch disability, mental health, and nutrition — all health equity issues. Being able to situate your empirical findings within the health equity framework (social determinants, upstream drivers, disparity vs. inequity) is expected in interviews and doctoral applications.
Domain Interview
Key readings: Braveman et al. (2011) — Health Disparities and Health Equity. Marmot & Wilkinson — Social Determinants of Health. Link & Phelan (1995) — Fundamental Causes. Follow RWJF's Commission to Build a Healthier America.
Critical
Disability Policy Landscape (ADA, Section 504, HCBS)
Developing
The ADA-PARC project gives you empirical credibility. To leverage it fully in interviews you need to be able to speak fluently about the policy landscape: the ADA, Olmstead decision, home and community-based services waivers, and current federal initiatives. This is what transforms analyst into expert.
Domain Interview
How to develop: ADA National Network Knowledge Translation Center. Disability Rights Advocates publications. Follow the National Council on Disability. Read the Olmstead decision and its implementation history.
High
Mental Health Policy & Public Health Framework
Developing
Your mood disorders paper applies a public health lens well. To develop this into a recurring portfolio theme, you need deeper familiarity with the policy landscape: SAMHSA programs, parity law, community mental health centers, and the evidence base for prevention vs. treatment.
Domain Portfolio
Key readings: SAMHSA reports and data. Kessler et al. on prevalence. McGinnis & Foege (1993) — Actual Causes of Death. Mental Health America state rankings. Track MHPAEA (parity law) implementation research.
High
Program Evaluation Frameworks (Logic Models, Theory of Change)
Learning
Most applied research roles — in government, nonprofits, or think tanks — involve program evaluation. Understanding how to construct a theory of change, read a logic model, and assess whether an evaluation design is adequate for the question is foundational.
Domain Methods Interview
How to develop: CDC's Introduction to Program Evaluation for Public Health (free). W.K. Kellogg Foundation Logic Model Development Guide. Practice building a logic model for one of your existing projects.
High

Domain
Key readings: William Julius Wilson — The Truly Disadvantaged. Robert Sampson — Great American City. Keeanga-Yamahtta Taylor — Race for Profit. CMAP and Metropolitan Planning Council local reports.
Medium
Nutrition & Food Security Policy

Your WIC project establishes a foothold here. Deepening this into a second substantive domain would strengthen the portfolio's thematic coherence: disability + health equity + nutrition all intersect in meaningful, researchable ways.
Domain

Research Process & Professional Practice

The infrastructure of a research career — habits, processes, and professional knowledge that compound over time.
High
Literature Review & Synthesis
Learning
Every project and every doctoral application requires situating your work in the existing literature. The skill is not just reading — it is identifying the gap your work fills, which requires knowing the conversation well enough to locate the open questions.
Methods Portfolio
How to develop: Use Zotero for reference management (free). Practice writing a 1-page literature gap memo before any new project. Ask: what do we know, what do we not know, why does that gap matter?
High
Research Question Formulation
Developing
The ability to formulate a tight, answerable, policy-relevant research question is the most underrated research skill. Most early-career analysts default to descriptive framing ("what is the prevalence of X?") when stronger questions are causal or comparative ("does X cause Y, and for whom?").
Methods Interview
How to develop: Practice the PICO or FINER framework for question formulation. For each project, write a one-sentence research question that is specific, answerable with available data, and policy-relevant.
High
Grant Writing & Research Proposal Drafting
Queued
Doctoral fellowships (NSF GRFP, Ford Foundation, Robert Wood Johnson) require competitive research proposals. Applied research roles often involve grant writing. Learning this form — specific aims, significance, innovation, approach — is career infrastructure.
Communication Portfolio
How to develop: Read NSF GRFP winning examples (available online). Read NIH's Specific Aims page guidance. Practice writing a 1-page specific aims page for any research question you care about.
Medium
IRB & Research Ethics
Queued
Any original data collection requires IRB review. Even secondary data work benefits from understanding the ethical framework — consent, confidentiality, vulnerable populations — because it shapes what you can and cannot claim about your data.
Methods
How to develop: Complete CITI training (free, widely recognized). UIC likely offers IRB training through the Office for the Protection of Research Subjects. Understand the difference between exempt, expedited, and full review.
Medium
Reference Management (Zotero)
Developing
A good citation manager is the difference between a sustainable research workflow and constant friction. Zotero integrates with Quarto and R Markdown, which means you can manage citations for your portfolio projects in the same tool as your academic writing.
Tools
How to develop: Zotero.org (free). Install the browser connector. Set up citation key rules. Learn the Quarto-Zotero integration for bibliography management in your site.
Ongoing
Conference Participation & Professional Network
Developing
Conferences are where you find doctoral programs, collaborators, mentors, and job leads — and where you practice articulating your work to strangers. For policy research: APPAM, APHA, APA, Urban Affairs Association. Presenting (even a poster) changes the experience entirely.
Interview Communication
Key venues: APPAM (Association for Public Policy Analysis & Management) — student travel awards available. APHA Annual Meeting. Urban Affairs Association. APA National Planning Conference (urban/disability focus).

Interview Intelligence

The meta-skills of presenting yourself — knowing what you know, knowing how to say it, and knowing what they're really asking.
Critical
Research Narrative (The "So What" Test)
Learning
For every project, you must be able to answer: "So what? Who cares? What should someone do differently because of this finding?" If you cannot answer this clearly in 30 seconds, the project does not help you in an interview. Practice until it's automatic.
Interview Communication
How to develop: Write a two-sentence "so what" statement for each project. Have someone outside your field read it and ask "why does this matter?" three times. Revise until the answer is immediate and satisfying.
Critical
Limitations Articulation (Methodological Honesty)
Learning
Every strong interview answer about research includes honest acknowledgment of limitations — and every weak one avoids them. Knowing your work's limitations is not a vulnerability; it is a sign of methodological maturity. Prepare one key limitation per project.
Interview Methods
How to develop: For each project: name the biggest threat to internal validity, the biggest threat to external validity, and the most important thing future research should do. Practice articulating this clearly in 3–4 sentences.
High
Research Agenda Articulation (Doctoral / Senior Roles)
Learning
Doctoral programs and senior research roles want to know not just what you've done but where you're going. A clear two-sentence research agenda statement — what you study, why, and with what approach — is expected in statements of purpose and senior role interviews.
Interview Portfolio
How to develop: Draft your research agenda statement now, even if imperfect. It should name: (1) the substantive domain, (2) the type of question you're most interested in, (3) the approach or method. Revise every 6 months.
High
Behavioral Interview Preparation (STAR Method)
Queued
Applied research and policy roles (government agencies, nonprofits, think tanks) often use behavioral interviews. Situation-Task-Action-Result answers are expected. Having 5–6 prepared stories from your work experience that can be adapted to any question is essential preparation.
Interview
How to develop: Write out 6 STAR stories: one about handling ambiguity in data, one about a methodological challenge, one about stakeholder communication, one about a time you disagreed with a framing, one about working under deadline, one about explaining technical work to a lay audience.
High
Policy Domain Current Events Fluency
Queued
Research roles expect you to situate your work in the current policy environment. If you're interviewing for a disability policy role and can't name the current administration's HCBS priorities or recent legislative developments, you signal low domain engagement regardless of your technical skills.
Interview Domain
How to develop: Subscribe to: Health Affairs blog, RWJF reports, Urban Wire (Urban Institute), KFF Health Policy News, NCD reports. Read weekly. Build a habit of connecting current events to your research domains.
Medium
Portfolio Presentation (Talking Through Work)
Queued
Many research interviews include a work sample review or portfolio walkthrough. Being able to pull up your site, navigate to a project, and narrate the key finding and method in real time — without fumbling — requires deliberate practice with your own portfolio.
Interview Portfolio
How to develop: Practice a 5-minute live walkthrough of your portfolio site with a friend. Navigate to each project page and narrate it as if explaining to a senior analyst who is evaluating your fit.
